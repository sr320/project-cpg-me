Germline DNA methylation history across invertebrates, estimated as CpG observed/expected (CpG O/E) from public genome annotations. Genes with low CpG O/E have lost CpGs to methylation-driven mutation over evolutionary time. Following Dimond & Roberts 2015.

What drives the axes

How this was done, and the caveats
  • Profiles. Distribution summary: median, mean, the two mixture modes (mean, SD, share), Ashman's D, log ΔBIC, GpC O/E median and the housekeeping vs inducible effect, each scaled by its median and MAD and clipped at ±3, so a degenerate mixture fit (a "mode" of one gene) cannot dominate. Full shape: the share of genes in each of the 80 CpG O/E bins. GO Slim: each term's median shift against other genes (missing terms set to 0). Shape and GO Slim are not rescaled. By default each GO Slim shift is weighted by how many genes it rests on: it is multiplied by τ²/(τ² + (π/2)σ²/n), where n is the term's gene count in that species, σ the typical per-gene SD of CpG O/E (median across species) and τ² the variance of that term's shift across species beyond what sampling noise explains. Shifts from few genes are pulled toward 0; a term whose spread is no larger than its noise drops out. Untick "Weight terms by gene count" for the raw shifts.
  • Fit. PCA is fitted on focal species only. Controls and the Lycorma transcriptome check are projected onto the same axes (hollow marks). Transcriptome species have CDS only, so they drop out of gene-body views.
  • Remove clade means. Subtracts each clade's average before the PCA, so the axes describe variation within clades. Use it to look past the large coral / bivalve / insect / tunicate differences.
  • Test. Invasive and comparison species are compared within each clade in the full profile space (all components, not just the two plotted): the distance between their centroids, combined across clades weighted by nanb/(na+nb). The p-value comes from 4,999 shuffles of the labels within clades, so clade differences cannot create a signal. It does not control relatedness inside a clade (e.g. many aphids), and invasion depends on opportunity as well as biology.
  • Invasion labels (manifest/traits.tsv) come from the IUCN Global Invasive Species Database (GISD) and the World Register of Introduced Marine Species (WRiMS), checked 2026-11-27 by scripts/check_invasion_dbs.py. Invasive: a GISD account describing the species as an alien, or at least one WRiMS locality flagged invasive or of concern. Introduced: non-native records but none flagged invasive. Native: a marine or brackish species with no non-native records. The bar for "invasive" is low: one flagged locality is enough, so Crassostrea virginica and Ostrea edulis count as invasive. Most insects and freshwater mussels are outside both databases; they keep a label drafted from general knowledge (source shown on hover). NEMESIS and CABI were not checked.

Does the gap survive GC content and gene length?

Housekeeping and inducible genes can differ in GC content and length, and both shape CpG O/E without any methylation. Here CpG O/E is first adjusted for GC content and length across all genes in the genome, and the housekeeping vs inducible comparison is repeated on what is left. If the gap came from composition or length, the adjusted effect would move to zero.

Rank-biserial correlation, housekeeping vs inducible genes, before and after adjustment. Below zero: housekeeping genes have lower CpG O/E.

Direct check with measured methylation (WGBS, gene body). Are housekeeping genes actually more methylated?

How this was done
  • Adjustment: over all genes passing the filter, CpG O/E is regressed on GC, GC², log length, its square and GC × log length. Housekeeping and inducible genes are then compared on the residuals with the same one-sided Mann-Whitney test and rank-biserial correlation as the original test.
  • Composition control: the same test on GpC O/E, which reflects base composition but not CpG loss to methylation. Hover a species to see it. With thousands of genes per species small GpC gaps can be significant, but they are much weaker than the CpG gap (at most about a third of it, in coral gene bodies) and often point the other way.
  • Why the adjustment can over-correct: methylated CpGs mutate to TpG over time, which lowers GC. Where heavily methylated genes are also AT-rich, as in the honey bee, adjusting for GC removes part of the methylation signal itself. The WGBS check avoids this: it compares measured methylation directly, and repeats that comparison after the same GC and length adjustment.
  • Expression breadth is not included: it needs RNA-seq across tissues for every species. Code: analysis/covariate_check.py.

Validation against WGBS

CpG O/E is an indirect estimate: it infers germline methylation from the CpGs a gene has lost over evolutionary time. Here it is checked against methylation measured directly by whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS) in germline tissue: oyster gonads and honey bee sperm. Each row compares, gene by gene, CpG O/E for one feature with the measured methylation level of the same gene in one sample.

Result: in all ten datasets, low CpG O/E picks out the methylated genes. Nearly every methylated gene falls in the low CpG O/E class, and genes in the high class are essentially unmethylated. The eight female gonads agree almost exactly.

How to read the columns
  • Genes: genes with at least 5 CpGs each covered by at least 5 reads. A gene's methylation is the share of methylated reads over those CpGs (0 to 1); it counts as methylated at 0.5 or more.
  • Spearman ρ: rank correlation between CpG O/E and measured methylation. Negative is expected (lower CpG O/E, more methylation); 0 would mean no relationship, −1 a perfect inverse ranking. Values near −0.7 to −0.8 are strong.
  • AUC: the chance that a randomly chosen methylated gene has lower CpG O/E than a randomly chosen unmethylated one. 0.5 is a coin toss, 1 is perfect separation.
  • Misclassified: share of genes where the CpG O/E class (low or high, from the two-component mixture) disagrees with measured methylation.
  • Precision: of genes in the low CpG O/E class, the share that are methylated. Recall: of methylated genes, the share in the low class.
  • Median meth, low / high class: typical measured methylation of genes in each class.

Recall is high (0.95 to 0.99) and precision lower (0.28 to 0.67), so the errors run one way: genes with low CpG O/E that are not methylated in the sequenced sample. That is expected. CpG O/E records methylation over evolutionary time, so some of those genes lost methylation recently or are methylated only in other cells or stages. Honey bee methylation is sparser and lighter (median 0.33 in the low class), which is why its precision is lowest. Hover a row for its data source.

Methods

  • Data. RefSeq annotated genomes from the NCBI Datasets API. CDS is the longest isoform per gene; gene body is the full gene span including introns; upstream is 1 kb before the gene on its strand. Features under 300 bp or with more than 5 % ambiguous bases are excluded.
  • CpG O/E = (nCG / (nC × nG)) × L² / (L − 1). GpC O/E is computed the same way as a composition control.
  • Mixtures. One- and two-component Gaussian mixtures per feature. A distribution is called bimodal when BIC favours two components by more than 10, Ashman's D (separation of the two modes) exceeds 2, and each component holds at least 5 % of genes (so a few extreme outliers cannot form a "mode").
  • Function. eggNOG-mapper 2.1.13 (eggNOG 5.0.2) on one protein per gene; GO terms mapped to GO Slim (generic, plus response to stress, translation and signal transduction). Housekeeping vs inducible: one-sided Mann-Whitney test; genes in both classes excluded.
  • Validation. WGBS reads aligned with Bismark to the same assembly; gene methylation is the weighted level over CpGs with ≥ 5 reads, for genes with ≥ 5 such CpGs.
  • Code, per-species outputs and the full write-up: github.com/sr320/project-cpg-me (PILOT.md).
